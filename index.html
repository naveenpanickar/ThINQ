Why ThINQ' — The Investment Case

Not a print catalogue. A capacity exchange. Physical brand production is a large, fragmented market run on phone calls and guesswork — with a requirement-to-capacity matching gap and near-zero tech penetration. That's the thesis.

Six Reasons Investors Find This Interesting

01Large, Fragmented Market

Enterprises still find production capacity by phone and referral. No category leader owns this. Spend is live, discovery is broken, tech penetration is near zero.

02Recurring Transaction Demand

Banks, retailers, hospitals, franchise chains — they never stop ordering brand materials. Predictable, repeat demand, compounded by the Asset Library reorder loop.

03Capacity Data Moat

Every job builds ThINQ' Score history, pricing benchmarks, and live capacity data. That intelligence compounds with every match — impossible for a late entrant to replicate.

04Multiple Revenue Levers

Transaction-based execution fee on every job · Category-specific execution charges as applicable · Capacity partner membership · Media Exchange commission · Managed services. No single-source dependency.

05Expansion Beyond Print

Sign, display, space, merch, mobility, media — eleven categories, one capacity engine. No rebuild required to add a category.

06Reorder Lock-in

Once a spec lives in the Asset Library with an approved partner and QC history, switching cost is high. Retention from the reorder loop — not just platform stickiness.

Investor Questions — Addressed
Is print a declining industry?
Not about print growth — it's capacity discovery. Enterprises already spend on physical execution across print, packaging, signage and more. ThINQ' improves how that demand finds capacity. Marketplace software for a stable-spend, undiscoverable category is a proven model.
You don't own any production — how do you guarantee quality?
Quality is enforced at three points, not assumed. Partners are onboarded with a verified Capacity Passport — equipment, materials, past QC record. Every job is checked against the approved spec before it ships, with photo proof logged. And a partner's ThINQ' Score — built from on-time delivery, defect rate and repeat-order approval — determines how much matched demand they see next. Bad output loses future work automatically; it doesn't need a support ticket to catch it.
Capacity Passport — vetted at onboarding Spec-matched QC — checked per job ThINQ' Score — earned, not claimed
What about Printo, Printstop, Gelato, or RRD?
They fulfil one category. ThINQ' exchanges capacity across eleven. Printo, Printstop, Gelato, RRD each own their own production and sell it directly. ThINQ' doesn't own presses — it routes demand across a qualified network. They're supply on the exchange, not the competition.
Printo / Printstop — retail fulfilment Gelato / RRD — owned production ThINQ' — capacity exchange
Can you sign buyers and capacity partners at the same time?
Buyer demand first. Capacity second. Secure enterprise accounts, then onboard qualified partners to serve committed demand. No speculative supply build — the classic marketplace cold-start failure, avoided by design.
We already work with local partners directly — why do we need ThINQ'?
Local partners solve one job. ThINQ' solves finding the right one, every time.
